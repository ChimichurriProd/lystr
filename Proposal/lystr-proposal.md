## Why you need this now (not later)

You already know most of this, but stated plainly so we're on the same page:

- **Your direct channel doesn't work.** Alex's own estimate is that ~5% of leads come through lystr.se. That's not a brand problem — it's a *business continuity* problem. If ETC changes terms, slows its list, or simply saturates, you have no second channel ready.
- **You have no measurement.** There is zero analytics on lystr.se. The "5% / 95%" split is operational intuition, not data. You cannot improve what you cannot measure, and you cannot defend growth numbers to investors without it.
- **Your site is a prototype.** It is one page on Framer, last updated 8 months ago, with two visible typos and no lead-capture form beyond a Calendly link. It is the single weakest artifact in your pipeline.
- **The investor conversation is coming.** You told us you need to raise capital to keep pace with growth. Any investor conducting basic diligence will look at lystr.se, read your explainer, and form a view in under 90 seconds. Today, that view is "early-stage, underbuilt."
- **The offer is unusually good but hard to understand quickly.** "8-year contract, you pay the same, get free energy for 35 years after" takes more than a sentence to explain. Your sales team compensates via phone calls. Your website does not, which is why it converts at 5%.

The window to fix this is short. Once you close a round, the cost of not having a credible public presence compounds — because now there's VC pressure to scale the direct channel, and you still don't have one.

## The business case

Let's talk numbers. You are signing ~70 installations a week. Each is worth 150–250k SEK over 8 years, with ~45k SEK in immediate cash from the grönt bidrag. Your reported revenue pace is ~18 MSEK per month and growing.

Today, roughly 5% of those 70 sign-ups come through direct channels — approximately **3–4 installations per week from lystr.se**. Your site, the sales deck, and the animation together are responsible for ~3.5 installations/week.

If the **Launch** redesign lifts that direct share to even 10% — from 3.5 to 7 installations per week — the incremental revenue is:

- **3.5 extra installations/week × 200k SEK/installation × 52 weeks = ~36 MSEK/year in new contract value**
- Immediate cash (grönt bidrag) from the incremental contracts: **3.5 × 45k × 52 = ~8.2 MSEK/year**

The **Launch** investment of ~710k (midpoint) pays back in under 2 months of incremental installations from the redesign, measured in cash-in-hand. Contract value over 8 years pays it back in the first 2–3 signed installations.

If the lift is smaller — say, 5% to 7% — you still recover the investment within 3–4 months.

**Phase 0 measurement is the thing that lets you prove this actually happened.** Without it, you can redesign the site and still not know if it helped. The 20k SEK we allocate to Phase 0 is non-negotiable precisely because it unlocks this entire business case.

## Why this is expensive (honestly)

You are going to look at the Launch number and think it's high. It is. Here's why:

- **A motion graphics explainer animation is 150–290k of the total.** Animation is labour-intensive: ~200 hours of senior creative work. We're quoting boutique Stockholm rates (1 000 SEK/h blended), which is half of what a larger agency would charge and twice what an offshore studio would charge. Agencies under 150k for this work either cut corners (stock templates, stock voice, no real storyboard) or are offshore. Both show.
- **A Next.js website that's not just a redesign but includes proper measurement, lead capture, and hand-off is 200–320k.** Framer builders charge 30–80k for a template-driven site; they can't build an interactive walkthrough or integrate a CRM pipeline without going custom, and once they do, they're at our price point.
- **Brand + deck + coordination is 150–220k.** This is where most "cheaper" agencies underdeliver — they do the logo and wordmark, ship a PDF, and move on. Proper brand work produces a system you can apply consistently across 50 future touchpoints.

The way to make this cheaper is to do less, not to do it worse. That's why **Pilot** exists — it does fewer things, but each thing it does is done properly.

## Our recommendation: Launch

You should take **Launch**. Here's why specifically, for Lystr's situation:

1. **The investor meeting is the forcing function.** The site, the animation, and the deck are the three artifacts an investor looks at before the first conversation. Pilot replaces only one of the three; Scale adds a marketing stack that's premature. Launch is the exact cross-section you need for a credible raise.
2. **The ETC dependency is a real business risk.** Launch is the minimum package that builds a parallel direct channel capable of scaling. Pilot patches the current channel; Scale assumes you already have a working direct channel to amplify — you don't.
3. **The ROI math is comfortable.** At 710k midpoint, Launch breaks even on ~4 extra signed contracts. Your current weekly volume is 70 total. A 10% direct-channel lift from the redesign delivers this in ~2 weeks of incremental conversion.
4. **The timing is compressed in a useful way.** 3 months is tight enough to keep the team focused, long enough to ship the full package. Pilot is faster but leaves two of the three artifacts unchanged. Scale is slower and asks you to commit to operational complexity you don't need yet.
