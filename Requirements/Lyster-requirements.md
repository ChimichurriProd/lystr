## 1. Background

Lyster is a new-generation energy company. The offer to the end customer is simple on the surface — *byt elavtal till oss* — and complex underneath: the customer signs an 8-year contract at roughly their current energy cost, and in exchange gets a solar installation, battery, service, insurance, and financing bundled in. The customer owns the installation after the contract ends and cuts their energy cost by ~65% for the remaining 35–50+ years of the system's life.

The offer works. Lyster has scaled from 18 MSEK/year as a solar company to 18 MSEK/month as an energy company, mostly via a piggyback partnership with ETC. The constraint now is that Lyster's own brand is underdeveloped — ~95% of leads come through ETC, ~5% through Lyster's own site. Lyster needs to make its own brand load-bearing so that (a) growth continues when the ETC channel saturates, (b) Lyster is credible when raising external capital, and (c) the offer can be understood by a customer in under a minute without a salesperson on the phone.

## 2. Target audience & tone

**Audience:** Swedish households, typically outside major cities, with thin margins. The reference customer is a family in Grimslöv outside Växjö. They cannot front 150 000 SEK, they are price-sensitive, and they are motivated primarily by lower and more predictable energy costs — climate and independence are secondary.

**Tone:** trygg, robust, trovärdig, lätt tillgänglig. Explicitly **not** "ung och hungrig / svart bakgrund / stora versaler". The brand should feel solid and serious — Lyster is asking the customer to commit for 8 years.

## 3. Scope of this engagement

Four deliverables, treated as a coordinated system so the customer experiences one story across video, web, and sales material.

### 3.1 Brand identity / graphic profile

Lyster has a logo (used on clothing and ETC co-branding) and no other brand assets — no color palette, no typography, no usage rules. The existing logo stays. This engagement defines everything around it: palette, typography, logo usage, graphic elements, tone of visual language, and a lightweight brand guidelines document.

**Constraint — Johan:** Johan is a ~60-year-old stakeholder who controls access to an intresseorganisation with ~800k members (~30k of which convert into ~600 MSEK/år in revenue potential). He wants to be involved in branding specifically — palette and logo direction. He cannot be removed from the process. The question is *how* Sriracha collaborates with him, and we propose three options for the customer to choose from:

- **Option A — Johan leads, we apply.** Johan delivers the brand direction (palette, logo treatment). Sriracha applies it consistently across the video, website, and deck. Lowest cost, lowest creative latitude, highest dependency risk (we wait on Johan).
- **Option B — Collaborative workshops.** Sriracha runs 2–3 working sessions with Johan, develops brand direction together, and delivers a documented system. Medium cost, shared ownership, Johan stays engaged.
- **Option C — Parallel proposals + alignment.** Sriracha develops a brand proposal in parallel with Johan, then the customer chooses or the two are merged. Highest cost, highest creative quality ceiling, most political risk with Johan.

**Customer decision required.** See open questions.

**Deliverables:**
- Logo usage rules (existing logo)
- Color palette (primary + secondary + functional)
- Typography system (headings, body, UI)
- Graphic elements / visual language
- Brand guidelines document ("lathund" — PDF, 10–20 pages)
- Digital asset kit (logos in SVG/PNG, fonts licensed or specified, color tokens)

### 3.2 Explainer video

A motion-graphics video that makes the offer understandable in one sitting. The customer (Alex) specifically asked for something that visualises the "pelar" — the three-part structure of the current energy cost (skatt + överföring + el), how solar and battery stabilise it during the 8-year contract, and how the cost drops ~65% after the contract ends and the installation is paid off.

The reference the customer liked (from competitor LV) is the tone and structure of the explanation — clear, calm, trovärdig. What they did *not* like was the dishonesty of LV's offer; the visual style is a neutral reference, not a copy target.

**Deliverables:**
- Script (developed collaboratively with Lyster — we bring a first draft, they edit)
- Storyboard
- Design frames
- Animation (motion graphics, ~60–90 seconds, target length to be confirmed)
- Sound design + voiceover (assuming Swedish voiceover; see open questions)
- Final video in web-ready formats (MP4 H.264, web-optimized; subtitles SRT)
- Social cutdowns **not included** in this scope — can be added

### 3.3 Interactive website (landing experience)

The customer asked for "halvt interaktivt" — a page where the visitor can explore the offer and reach the same understanding the video gives them, but at their own pace. The narrative is the same as the video; the format is different.

**Current state:** Lyster has an existing website. ~5% of current leads come through it. The customer called it "fruktansvärt" — it was built in-house by Alex. This engagement can either replace it, extend it, or build a dedicated landing experience alongside it.

**Deliverables (assuming dedicated landing experience, one primary page + necessary supporting pages):**
- Strategy & sitemap
- Interaction design (the exploratory walkthrough)
- Visual design
- Copy (first draft by Sriracha, Lyster reviews/approves)
- Development
- Integration with lead-capture form (hand-off to whatever CRM/email system Lyster uses — see open questions)
- Responsive (mobile + desktop)
- Launch + handover

**Out of scope unless separately requested:** full multi-page site replacement, blog, customer portal, admin dashboards.

### 3.4 PowerPoint sales deck

A reusable deck that tells the same story as the video, for sales use — account management, investor conversations, partner meetings. Master template + one populated example deck (~15–25 slides). Single light variant (unlike Lumiverse, no dark mode requested).

## 6. Open questions — need to be answered before sign-off

These items must be clarified by Lyster (or Sriracha in conversation with Lyster) before this document can be treated as final. We propose a kickoff meeting to resolve them in one sitting.

### 6.1 Brand collaboration with Johan (Section 3.1)
- Which option (A, B, or C) does Lyster want?
- Who owns final creative sign-off if Johan and Sriracha disagree?

### 6.2 Accounts & tooling ownership (applies across all deliverables)
The meeting implied that Sriracha would set things up using Lyster's own accounts (domains, hosting, email, analytics, CMS, etc.). This needs confirmation:
- Does Lyster have existing accounts for: domain registrar, web hosting, DNS, analytics, form/CRM, email marketing, font licensing?
- Which of these does Sriracha access, and under what credentials (shared, delegated, SSO)?
- Who pays for recurring subscriptions (hosting, fonts, tooling) — Lyster directly, or invoiced through Sriracha?
- Post-launch: does Sriracha retain admin access for maintenance, or fully hand over?

**Our recommendation:** Lyster owns all accounts and pays subscriptions directly. Sriracha is granted delegated access during the project and an agreed maintenance window after launch. This avoids lock-in and clarifies IT ownership.

### 6.3 Website scope (Section 3.3)
- Replace existing site entirely, or build a dedicated landing page alongside it?
- Preferred tech stack / CMS — or is Sriracha free to choose? (We recommend Next.js on Vercel with a headless CMS for speed and easy handover.)
- Which CRM / lead-capture does the form submit to? (HubSpot? Mailchimp? Something custom? The ETC flow today?)
- Does Lyster need an admin interface for non-technical staff to edit copy, or is a developer-edit workflow acceptable initially?

### 6.4 Explainer video (Section 3.2)
- Target length — 60s, 90s, 120s? (We recommend 75–90s.)
- Voiceover — professional Swedish VO talent, or internal voice (Alex)? Budget for VO included separately below.
- Music — licensed library track, or custom composition?
- Language — Swedish only, or also English version for investor use?

### 6.5 Timeline (Section 5)
- What is the actual deadline or target launch date?
- Are there any fixed events (investor meeting, press moment, capital-raise close) that the deliverables must be ready for?
- Phased launch acceptable (e.g., brand + deck first, website + video second), or all-at-once?

### 6.6 Rights & usage (applies to video, brand, deck, website)
When Sriracha creates original work, there are two common arrangements:
- **Buyout:** Lyster owns the final assets outright and can use them anywhere, forever, with no further payment. Typically costs more up front.
- **License:** Sriracha retains ownership; Lyster licenses the work for defined uses (e.g., "web and social, 3 years"). Cheaper up front but with restrictions.

**Our recommendation:** full buyout of final delivered assets, with Sriracha retaining portfolio and case-study rights. This is the cleanest arrangement for a growing brand.

Additional usage considerations:
- Font licensing — who holds the license (Lyster)? Any constraint on font choice from Johan?
- Stock imagery / music — who pays and who holds the license?
- Source files — are editable project files (AE, Figma, PPTX source) delivered to Lyster at the end, or held by Sriracha?
