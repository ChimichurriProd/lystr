# International peers & design inspiration for lystr.se

Researched 2026-04-24. Focused on companies doing something structurally close to Lystr — solar + battery + no/low upfront cost + multi-year contract — and on Nordic energy brands with high design maturity.

---

## TL;DR — the three sites most worth studying

1. **Enpal** (Germany) — [enpal.de](https://www.enpal.de) — the closest business-model match in Europe. Mature visual language, calculator-in-hero, 120 000 customers, heavy use of trust signals. If Lystr had a 5-years-older sibling, it would be Enpal.
2. **Sunsave Plus** (UK) — [sunsave.energy](https://sunsave.energy) — the UK's first solar subscription. Most tonally similar to where Lystr wants to land: friendly but credible, concrete saving numbers, 4-step visual process, yellow accents (not red).
3. **Tibber** (Norway / Sweden / Germany) — [tibber.com](https://tibber.com/en) — not a solar-bundle peer, but the gold standard for Nordic energy-tech design. Contrarian voice, minimalist, electric blue. Read it for tone, not for model.

Everything below is detail supporting those three.

## Patterns observed across these sites

### Hero structure that works

Four formulas appear repeatedly:

1. **Cost anchor.** The number in the first sentence. "0 € Anzahlung" (Enpal), "$0 upfront" (Sunrun, Sunsave), "Save £27/month" (Sunsave). Works because the reader's core question is "can I afford this?" and you answer it before they ask.
2. **Vision statement.** "To live on wind and sunlight forever for free" (1KOMMA5°). Useful when you want investor/values signaling alongside consumer conversion. High-risk — easy to read as pretentious.
3. **Contrarian positioning.** "Forget everything you know about energy companies" (Tibber). Works when the incumbent is explicitly the enemy. Lystr has the same opportunity ("The electricity companies don't want you to own your energy").
4. **Direct benefit.** "All your solar & battery needs under one roof" (Octopus). Plain, descriptive, hard to screw up.

Lystr's current "Läs mer om ditt livs bästa investering" is closer to formula 4 but weaker — it tells you to *learn more*, not what the benefit *is*.

### Primary CTA — the "book a call" problem

**None of these sites make booking a call the primary CTA.** Every one of them leads with a lower-commitment entry point:

- **Calculator-in-hero** (Enpal, Sunsave, Palmetto) — "Calculate my savings" takes the visitor through 3-6 questions and produces a personalised estimate. Converts more visitors than a calendar. This is the single biggest pattern worth stealing.
- **Postcode-entry form** (Sunrun, Octopus) — "Enter your postcode" qualifies location and gets the visitor to the next step before asking for email.
- **"Get a quote" button** (Sunrun again, as a secondary path).

Lystr today makes "Boka möte" the primary CTA. That's equivalent to asking every first-time visitor for an hour of their life. The dropoff is predictable.

### Trust signals — five things everyone does

1. **Customer count.** "120 000+ households" (Enpal, 1KOMMA5°), "1 million homes" (Sunrun). This is the single most repeated trust marker.
2. **Press / test-winner logos.** Enpal stacks them aggressively: CHIP, home&smart, Süddeutsche Zeitung, ZDF, Focus. Sunsave shows TechCrunch, Telegraph, Sky News.
3. **Partner logos.** Sunsave displays Octopus and Hive. Lystr could display Cleansun + ETC here.
4. **Government/standards backing.** "TÜV-certified," "Which? Trusted Trader," "£2.2m from UK government." For Lystr: grönt bidrag eligibility, elcertifikat, elinstallatör-licens.
5. **Geographic diversity in testimonials.** Enpal specifically shows customer photos from different parts of Germany. Tangibly de-risks for the visitor — "someone like me has done this."

### Photography over illustration

Enpal, 1KOMMA5°, Sunrun, Sunsave, Octopus — all lead with **real photography** of real homes and real customers. Illustration shows up as supporting (icons, small spot illustrations), not as the primary visual language.

Palmetto and Tibber are the exceptions — both use more illustration, and both are more tech-positioning brands (AI, app).

**Lystr's current imagery** (Brand/lystr/images/) is heavy on golden-hour Nordic nature stock. That's not what peers use. Peers use installation photography + customer portraits.

### Colour palettes observed

- **Blue dominant**: Enpal (navy + orange), Sunrun, Palmetto, Tibber (electric blue accent on white)
- **Yellow accent**: Sunsave (yellow checkmarks/badges on white)
- **Pink primary**: Octopus (but muted on the solar subpage)
- **Near-black + single accent**: 1KOMMA5°, Otovo

**No site we saw used red as the primary accent.** Lystr's current `#E6414F` red reads as "alert/warning" in the utility context — worth reconsidering. Viable alternatives:
- **Electric blue** (Tibber-adjacent, but already dominant in the category)
- **Orange/amber** (Enpal-adjacent, warmth + energy, distinct from solar yellow)
- **Leaf green** (eco, but risks the "climate-first" framing Alex doesn't want)

### No gradients

Confirms Alex's brief. Zero major competitors use prominent gradient treatments. Flat, solid, confident.

## Specific tactics worth stealing for lystr.se

In rough priority order.

### 1. Replace "Anmäl ditt intresse" with "Räkna ut din besparing"

Put a 3-6 question calculator in the hero. Collect postnummer, current monthly bill, house type, roof direction. Output: estimated monthly cost with Lystr + estimated savings vs current. End with email capture to send a PDF estimate + book a call.

Copies Enpal's strongest conversion pattern. Captures the 80% of visitors not ready to book a call — which is most of them.

### 2. Concrete number in hero, not a benefit claim

Today: *"Läs mer om ditt livs bästa investering"*
Better: something like *"Samma elräkning. Egen energiproduktion. I 30 år."* or *"Byt elavtal. Behåll ekonomin. Få din egen solanläggning."*

Sunsave's *"Save £27/month on your £100/month bill"* is the conversion-proof pattern. Adapt with an average Swedish elräkning.

### 3. The 3- or 4-step process diagram

Sunsave's and Octopus's "postcode → tailored quote → installation" is a staple. For Lystr: *Intresseanmälan → Projektering → Installation → Din anläggning*. Solves the "this sounds complicated" reaction in five seconds.

### 4. Customer count near the hero

"Över XXXX svenska hushåll har tecknat avtal med Lystr" — right under the hero. If the number is big, it's a flex; if it's small, it's still growing-credibility; either way better than nothing.

### 5. Installation photography

The current site leans on abstract nature imagery. Invest in real installation photos — Swedish houses, Swedish customers, Swedish roofs. Enpal spends real money here because it's the single highest-ROI visual asset.

### 6. Side-by-side cost comparison

From Alex's own pitch: *"Nuvarande elkostnad vs. Lystr-kostnad över 8 år och efter 8 år"*. This is already the backbone of the offer; make it the backbone of the page too. Sunsave does it with a simple monthly saving callout; Lystr can do it with the more sophisticated 8-year / post-contract stacked bar the animation is likely to use.

### 7. Contrarian voice option

If Alex is comfortable being a little bolder: *"Elbolagen tjänar på att du inte äger din energi. Vi tjänar på att du gör det."* Tibber-style. Works for the "stärker totalförsvaret" and anti-utility elements already in the current copy. High-risk if too edgy, but Alex explicitly said the brand should feel "trovärdig" — not "tame." Worth testing in two mockups.

### 8. Rethink the red

The current `#E6414F` reads as alarm in energy context (overconsumption warnings, overload indicators). Consider:
- A deeper, warmer **amber/orange** (closer to Enpal, warmth of the sun without being literal yellow)
- An **electric blue** (closer to Tibber, tech-forward)
- **Deep green** as accent with a warm off-white as surface (closer to "soft" Nordic brands)

Not saying red is wrong. Saying it deserves a second look in brand discovery.

## Suggested moodboard (show to Alex in kickoff)

Print or screenshot these four sections and walk Alex through them. Ask him which feels like Lystr:

1. **Enpal homepage hero** — the fully-loaded cost-anchor + calculator approach
2. **Sunsave's "Save £27/month" section** — concrete math as the hero visual
3. **Tibber homepage** — minimalist Nordic voice
4. **1KOMMA5°'s impact-metrics-led hero** — CO₂ savings, household count, climate framing

If he leans toward (1) or (2): we build a calculator-first conversion-focused site.
If he leans toward (3): we lean into voice, less on conversion mechanics, and compensate with the lead form.
If he leans toward (4): we sell the scale and the movement, less the product.

The requirements doc currently assumes something between (1) and (3). The moodboard conversation will narrow it.
